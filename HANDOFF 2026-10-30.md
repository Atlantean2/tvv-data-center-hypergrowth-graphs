## 1. `Hyperscaler and CoreWeave debt outstanding 2025-2026.png` → **Q3 Financing**
- **Title:** Hyperscaler bond binge: Amazon, Alphabet and Meta debt up ~$280B in a year
- **Caption:** Long-term debt by quarter; Amazon, Alphabet, Meta added ~$281B, Sep 2025–Sep 2026. Source: SEC 10-Q XBRL.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - Sep 30 2026 vs Sep 30 2025:
    - Amazon $180.9B vs $55.1B
    - Alphabet $125.1B (noncurrent) vs $21.6B
    - Meta $80.9B vs $28.8B
  - Oracle had $125.3B of notes and loans at Aug 31 2026, down from a $134.6B peak in Feb 2026. CoreWeave had $35.6B of debt at Jun 30 2026, up from $8.0B at Dec 2024.
  - Microsoft is the outlier, paying debt down to $35.5B.
- **Sources**
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001018724.json
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001652044.json
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001326801.json
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001341439.json
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001769628.json
  - https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000040/amzn-20260930.htm
- **Doubt notes**
  - The XBRL tags differ by company:
    - Amazon and Microsoft: LongTermDebt
    - Alphabet and Meta: LongTermDebtNoncurrent; their current portions are small, about $1.0B and $2.75B

  - Values are carrying amounts, not face value.
  - "These firms can easily service this debt." That's true for Amazon, Alphabet and Meta (see the CoreWeave/Oracle burden chart for the contrast). The risk is the speed of the increase, not solvency.

## 2. `Meta non-cancelable contractual commitments 2025-2026.png` → **Q4 Off-Balance-Sheet Exposure**
- **Title:** Meta's non-cancelable commitments jumped from $28B to $455B in five quarters
- **Caption:** Meta's off-balance-sheet purchase commitments reached $454.5B at Sep 30 2026, separate from $355.7B of uncommenced leases. Source: Meta 10-Q.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - Commitments were $27.95B at Jun 30 2025 and $454.52B at Sep 30 2026, mostly third-party cloud capacity, servers, network gear and energy agreements.
  - Of that, $45.85B is due in the rest of 2026 and $121.41B in 2027. There are also up to $14.72B of contingent cloud purchases.
  - Separately, Meta has $355.7B of leases signed but not commenced (2026–2036), also off the balance sheet.
  - Amazon comparison, Sep 30 2026: total commitments $883.6B. About $438B of that is not on the balance sheet (leases not commenced $164.9B, property and equipment $159.6B, purchase obligations $94.8B, other $19.0B).
- **Sources**
  - https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026066773/meta-20260930.htm
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001326801.json
  - https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000040/amzn-20260930.htm
- **Doubt notes**
  - Don't add the $454.5B and the $355.7B to the existing ~$1.2T uncommenced-lease chart (06) without checking for overlap. Chart 06 already counts Meta's lease figure; this chart is the separate commitments line.
  - "Commitments are normal business." The 16-fold jump in five quarters is the notable part, not the existence of commitments.

## 3. `CoreWeave and Oracle interest expense as share of revenue 2024-2026.png` → **Q5 Credit Stress**
- **Title:** CoreWeave spends 22¢ of every revenue dollar on interest; Oracle 7.4¢
- **Caption:** Quarterly interest expense as % of revenue: CoreWeave 21.7% (Q2 2026), Oracle 7.4% (Aug 2026 quarter). Source: SEC XBRL.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - CoreWeave Q2 2026 (to Jun 30): interest $0.558B on revenue $2.575B, and operating income was −$0.049B. Interest is not covered by operating profit.
  - Oracle quarter ended Aug 31 2026: interest $1.428B on revenue $19.345B (7.4%), with operating income $6.73B, so it is covered about 4.7×.
  - Oracle's quarterly interest has risen from $0.84B (Aug 2024) to $1.43–1.44B.
- **Sources**
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001769628.json
  - https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001341439.json
  - https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001769628&type=10-Q
- **Doubt notes**
  - Q4 values are derived as the full year minus the 9-month total (CoreWeave Dec 2025: $0.351B; Oracle May 2026: $1.439B).
  - The tags are InterestExpenseDebt for CoreWeave and InterestExpense for Oracle. Capitalized interest is excluded, so true borrowing cost is higher.
  - CoreWeave's Q3 2026 10-Q is not filed yet; update it when it lands, around mid-Nov.

## 4. `PJM capacity auction cost and price 2018-2029.png` → **NEW Q7 Power & Grid**
- **Title:** PJM grid capacity bill: $2.2B to $16.4B a year; data centers drove $6.3B of the latest
- **Caption:** PJM 2028/29 capacity cost $16.4B at the $325/MW-day cap; IMM: $6.3B from data centers. Sources: PJM, IMM.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - Jul 14 2026: the 2028/29 auction cleared at the $325/MW-day cap, $16.4B in total, 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement.
  - The simulated no-cap price was $554.72/MW-day, which would have meant $29.7B.
  - Jul 28 2026, IMM: data-center load accounts for $6.3B (38.2%) of the 2028/29 cost and $29.4B (46.2%) over the last four auctions.
  - The data-center load forecast rose from 7.9 GW (2025/26) to 21.5 GW (2028/29).
- **Sources**
  - https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/markets-ops/rpm/rpm-auction-info/2028-2029/2028-2029-bra-results-report.pdf
  - https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2026-releases/20260714-pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138318-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf
  - https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/presentations/2026/IMM_MC_Market_Monitor_Report_20260728.pdf
- **Doubt notes**
  - The IMM's data-center attribution is a counterfactual, a re-run of the auction without data-center load. PJM itself doesn't publish that split.
  - Prices are capped by a settlement collar, so the real scarcity signal is higher, not lower.
- **Substitution:** the EIA API needs a key (it returned 403), so ERCOT and EIA queue/load data were not pulled; PJM was used.

## 5. `US data center electricity use and share LBNL 2014-2028.png` → **NEW Q7 Power & Grid**
- **Title:** US data centers could use 6.7–12% of all US electricity by 2028, up from 4.4%
- **Caption:** US data-center use was 176 TWh (4.4%) in 2023; the 2028 scenarios are 325–580 TWh (6.7–12%). Source: LBNL/DOE, Dec 2024.
- **Label:** DATA (2014–2023) / PROJECTION (2028, hatched bar)
- **Bullets**
  - US data-center use was 58 TWh in 2014, 76 TWh (1.9%) in 2018 and 176 TWh (4.4%) in 2023.
  - The 2028 scenarios are 325–580 TWh, equal to 6.7–12.0% of US electricity and 74–132 GW.
  - Published Dec 20 2024 by LBNL for DOE. This is the most recent official US-wide estimate.
- **Sources**
  - https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report_1.pdf
  - https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers
- **Doubt notes**
  - "Efficiency gains will cap demand." The low scenario already assumes them. IEA's view is in the existing chart 01: https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai
  - The 2014 share was not stated in the summary sources used, so no 2014 share is labelled.
